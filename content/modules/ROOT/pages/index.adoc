= Introduction to the Lab

The move to software-defined vehicles (SDVs) in the automotive industry presents challenges in developing software for electronic control units (ECUs) that
require hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) testing.

This lab shows you how a "Software Factory" approach supports the development of automotive software. It uses the
*Red Hat Automotive Suite* (RHAS), an integrated development environment on Red Hat OpenShift for developing, testing,
and deploying applications for the
https://www.redhat.com/en/blog/new-standard-red-hat-vehicle-operating-system-modern-and-future-vehicles[Red Hat In-Vehicle OS,window=_blank]
(RHIVOS).

You will use the components of the suite: you work in
https://developers.redhat.com/products/openshift-dev-spaces/overview[Red Hat OpenShift Dev Spaces,window=_blank], lease a
virtual device with https://jumpstarter.dev/[Jumpstarter,window=_blank], boot a RHIVOS image with containerized
automotive applications on it, and look inside the running system. You will also see how Jumpstarter and the RHAS
Builder work together with
https://www.redhat.com/en/technologies/cloud-computing/openshift/pipelines[Red Hat OpenShift Pipelines,window=_blank]
to build and test every change automatically.

== Lab activities and objectives

This is a guided lab. You do not have to write code: you follow the steps and see each part of the
platform in action.

* Log in to your personal workspace and connect it to the build service and to Jumpstarter
* Lease a virtual device, flash a RHIVOS image onto it, and boot it
* Explore the running system: the safety-relevant root partition, the QM partition, and the containerized applications in both
* See how SELinux labels control which applications may communicate across the partitions
* Let an application in the QM partition run out of memory, and see that the safety-relevant applications are not affected
* Find out how the image you booted was built: the image manifest and the automated build pipeline

== How the lab runs

. *Introduction* - All participants start together with a short introduction to the platform, the demo application,
  and the idea of freedom from interference.
. *Hands-on* - You work through the lab activities in your own workspace, at your own pace. Instructors are in the room to help.
  This part takes about 60 minutes.
. *Demonstrations* - Your instructors show, on the projector, what does not fit into a hands-on lab: a change running
  through the build pipeline, the same image on a physical ARM board, and freedom from interference under CPU load.

Sections marked *Bonus* are optional. Skip them if you are short on time, and come back to them later.

At the end of this lab, you will have a basic understanding of the Red Hat Automotive Suite and how it helps you develop, build, and test automotive software.
